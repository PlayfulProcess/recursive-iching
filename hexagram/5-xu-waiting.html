Hexagram 5 of 64 · King Wen order

需Waiting

Xū · ䷄ · above: Water ☵ 坎 Kǎn · below: Heaven ☰ 乾 Qián

It indicates a time of inner strength and outward patience, waiting for the right moment for action while maintaining composure.A one-line gloss written for this site (AI-assisted). Everything below it is the classical text.

The Judgment 卦辭

有孚，光亨，貞吉。利涉大川。

Hsü intimates that, with the sincerity which is declared in it, there will be brilliant success. With firmness there will be good fortune; and it will be advantageous to cross the great stream.

James Legge, The Yî King (1882), the Text. Words in (parentheses) are Legge's own additions.

Commentary on the Judgment 彖傳 (Tuan, the first Wing)

1. Hsu denotes waiting. (The figure) shows peril in front; but notwithstanding the firmness and strength (indicated by the inner trigram), its subject does not allow himself to be involved (in the dangerous defile);—it is right he should not be straitened or reduced to extremity.

2. When it is said that, 'with the sincerity declared in Hsu, there will be brilliant success, and with firmness there will be good fortune,' this is shown by the position (of the fifth line) in the place assigned by Heaven, and its being the correct position for it, and in the centre. 'It will be advantageous to go through the great stream;'—that is, going forward will be followed by meritorious achievement.

The Image 象

雲上於天，需；君子以飲食宴樂。

(The trigram for) clouds ascending over that for the sky forms Hsü. The superior man, in accordance with this, eats and drinks, feasts and enjoys himself (as if there were nothing else to employ him).

Legge's Great Image (Appendix II). The Chinese above also carries the Small Images of the six lines.

The six lines 爻辭

Read from the bottom (line 1) to the top (line 6). Each line: the Chinese, Legge's translation, and the Small Image commentary on it.

Line 1 初九

The first line from the bottom, yang (solid, nine)

需于郊。利用恆，無咎。

The first NINE, undivided, shows its subject waiting in the distant border. It will be well for him constantly to maintain (the purpose thus shown), in which case there will be no error.

Small Image: 'He is waiting in the (distant) border:'—he makes no movement to encounter rashly the difficulties (of the situation). 'It will be advantageous for him constantly to maintain (the purpose thus shown), in which case there will be no error:'—he will not fail to pursue that regular course.

If this line changes, it becomes Hexagram 48, The Well.

Line 2 九二

The second line from the bottom, yang (solid, nine)

需于沙。小有言，終吉。

The second NINE, undivided, shows its subject waiting on the sand (of the mountain stream). He will (suffer) the small (injury of) being spoken (against), but in the end there will be good fortune.

Small Image: 'He is waiting on the sand:'—he occupies his position in the centre with a generous forbearance. Though 'he suffer the small injury of being spoken (against),' he will bring things to a good issue.

If this line changes, it becomes Hexagram 63, After Completion.

Line 3 九三

The third line from the bottom, yang (solid, nine)

需于泥，致寇至。

The third NINE, undivided, shows its subject in the mud (close by the stream). He thereby invites the approach of injury.

Small Image: 'He is waiting in the mud:'—calamity is (close at hand, and as it were) in the outer (trigram). 'He himself invites the approach of injury:'—if he be reverent and careful, he will not be worsted.

If this line changes, it becomes Hexagram 60, Limitation.

Line 4 六四

The fourth line from the bottom, yin (broken, six)

需于血，出自穴。

The fourth SIX, divided, shows its subject waiting in (the place of) blood. But he will get out of the cavern.

Small Image: 'He is waiting in (the place of) blood:'—he accommodates himself (to the circumstances of the time), and hearkens to (its requirements).

If this line changes, it becomes Hexagram 43, Break-through.

Line 5 九五

The fifth line from the bottom, yang (solid, nine)

需于酒食，貞吉。

The fifth NINE, undivided, shows its subject waiting amidst the appliances of a feast. Through his firmness and correctness there will be good fortune.

Small Image: 'The appliances of a feast, and the good fortune through being firm and correct,' are indicated by (the position in) the central and correct place.

If this line changes, it becomes Hexagram 11, Peace.

Line 6 上六

The sixth line from the bottom, yin (broken, six)

入于穴，有不速之客三人來，敬之終吉。

The topmost SIX, divided, shows its subject entered into the cavern. (But) there are three guests coming, without being urged, (to his help). If he receive them respectfully, there will be good fortune in the end.

Small Image: 'Guests come unurged (to give their help), and if (the subject of the line) receive them respectfully, there will be good fortune in the end:'—though the occupant and the place are not suited to each other, there has been no great failure (in what has been done).

If this line changes, it becomes Hexagram 9, The Taming Power of the Small.
