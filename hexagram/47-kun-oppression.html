Hexagram 47 of 64 · King Wen order

困Oppression

Kùn · ䷮ · above: Lake ☱ 兌 Duì · below: Water ☵ 坎 Kǎn

A trial of character during times of restriction; true strength is shown by remaining cheerful in adversity.A one-line gloss written for this site (AI-assisted). Everything below it is the classical text.

The Judgment 卦辭

亨，貞，大人吉，無咎，有言不信。

In (the condition denoted by) Khwăn there may (yet be) progress and success. For the firm and correct, the (really) great man, there will be good fortune. He will fall into no error. If he make speeches, his words cannot be made good.

James Legge, The Yî King (1882), the Text. Words in (parentheses) are Legge's own additions.

Commentary on the Judgment 彖傳 (Tuan, the first Wing)

1. In Khwan (we see) the strong (lines) covered and obscured (by the weak).

2. We have in it (the attribute of) perilousness going on to that of satisfaction. Who is it but the superior man that, though straitened, still does not fail in making progress to his proper end?

'For the firm and correct, the (really) great man, there will be good fortune:'—this is shown by the central positions of the strong (lines).

'If he make speeches, his words cannot be made good:'—to be fond of arguing or pleading is the way to be reduced to extremity.

The Image 象

澤無水，困；君子以致命遂志。

(The trigram representing) a marsh, and (below it that for a defile, which has drained the other dry so that there is) no water in it, form Khwăn. The superior man, in accordance with this, will sacrifice his life in order to carry out his purpose.

Legge's Great Image (Appendix II). The Chinese above also carries the Small Images of the six lines.

The six lines 爻辭

Read from the bottom (line 1) to the top (line 6). Each line: the Chinese, Legge's translation, and the Small Image commentary on it.

Line 1 初六

The first line from the bottom, yin (broken, six)

臀困于株木，入于幽谷，三歲不覿。

The first SIX, divided, shows its subject with bare buttocks straitened under the stump of a tree. He enters a dark valley, and for three years has no prospect (of deliverance).

Small Image: 'He enters a dark valley:'—so benighted is he, and without clear vision.

If this line changes, it becomes Hexagram 58, The Joyous, Lake.

Line 2 九二

The second line from the bottom, yang (solid, nine)

困于酒食，朱紱方來，利用亨祀，征凶，無咎。

The second NINE, undivided, shows its subject straitened amidst his wine and viands. There come to him anon the red knee-covers (of the ruler). It will be well for him (to maintain his sincerity as) in sacrificing. Active operations (on his part) will lead to evil, but he will be free from blame.

Small Image: 'He is straitened amidst his wine and viands:'—(but) his position is central, and there will be ground for congratulation.

If this line changes, it becomes Hexagram 45, Gathering Together.

Line 3 六三

The third line from the bottom, yin (broken, six)

困于石，據于蒺藜，入于其宮，不見其妻，凶。

The third SIX, divided, shows its subject straitened before a (frowning) rock. He lays hold of thorns. He enters his palace, and does not see his wife. There will be evil.

Small Image: 'He lays hold of thorns:'—(this is suggested by the position of the line) above the strong (line). 'He enters his palace, and does not see his wife:'—this is inauspicious.

If this line changes, it becomes Hexagram 28, Preponderance of the Great.

Line 4 九四

The fourth line from the bottom, yang (solid, nine)

來徐徐，困于金車，吝，有終。

The fourth NINE, undivided, shows its subject proceeding very slowly (to help the subject of the first line), who is straitened by the carriage adorned with metal in front of him. There will be occasion for regret, but the end will be good.

Small Image: 'He proceeds very slowly (to help the subject of the first line):'—his aim is directed to (help) that lower (line). Although he is not in his appropriate place, he and that other will (in the end) be together.

If this line changes, it becomes Hexagram 29, The Abysmal.

Line 5 九五

The fifth line from the bottom, yang (solid, nine)

劓刖，困于赤紱，乃徐有說，利用祭祀。

The fifth NINE, undivided, shows its subject with his nose and feet cut off. He is straitened by (his ministers in their) scarlet aprons. He is leisurely in his movements, however, and is satisfied. It will be well for him to be (as sincere) as in sacrificing (to spiritual beings).

Small Image: 'His nose and feet are cut off:'—his aim has not yet been gained. 'He is leisurely, however, in his movements, and is satisfied:'—his position is central and (his virtue) is correct. 'It will be well for him to be (as sincere as) in sacrificing:'— so shall he receive blessing.

If this line changes, it becomes Hexagram 40, Deliverance.

Line 6 上六

The sixth line from the bottom, yin (broken, six)

困于葛藟，于臲卼，曰動悔。有悔，征吉。

The sixth SIX, divided, shows its subject straitened, as if bound with creepers; or in a high and dangerous position, and saying (to himself), 'If I move, I shall repent it.' If he do repent of former errors, there will be good fortune in his going forward.

Small Image: 'He is straitened as if bound with creepers:'—(his spirit and action) are unsuitable. '(He says), "If I move, I shall repent of it." And he does repent (of former errors), which leads to good fortune:'—so he (now) goes on.

If this line changes, it becomes Hexagram 6, Conflict.
