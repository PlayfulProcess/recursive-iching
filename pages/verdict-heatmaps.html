Auspicious and ominous: the verdict words

The 384 line texts of the Zhouyi, read in the classical Chinese, hold a handful of verdict words: 吉 (auspicious), 凶 (ominous), 悔 (regret), 吝 (humiliation), 咎 (blame, mostly 無咎, no blame) and 厲 (danger). Each cell below is the share of the texts in that cell that hold the word, with the count beside it. Colour runs from warm (more 凶 than 吉) through paper (even) to cool (more 吉 than 凶), and every cell prints its numbers, so nothing depends on colour. Tap a hexagram to read its six lines.

What stands out, computed

The clearest pattern is the place: the central lines (2 and 5) carry 吉 in 44% of their 128 texts against 24% of the other 256, and 凶 in 7% against 17% (p = 0.00016 and p = 0.0073, exact Fisher).

Solid or broken matters much less. In the fifth place the broken (yin) line carries 吉 in 17 of 32 texts (53%) and the solid (yang) line in 12 of 32 (38%), yet with 32 texts a side that gap does not separate them (p = 0.32).

Lines in a correct place (yang in 1, 3, 5; yin in 2, 4, 6) carry 吉 in 30% of 192 texts, no more than lines in an incorrect place (32%).

These are counts of words, a pattern to test and not a rule. 貞吉 ("firmness brings good fortune") is a condition, 悔亡 ("regret vanishes") holds 悔, and one text can hold both 吉 and 凶. The p-values are not adjusted for the many cuts on this page, and the 384 texts are not independent draws.

How it is counted

  • The texts. The 384 line texts (64 hexagrams, 6 lines) in the classical Chinese of grammars/i-ching-chinese-original (Project Gutenberg #25501, public domain). The Judgments and the Wings are not counted.
  • The words. A text is counted once for each word it holds. 無咎 is also counted on its own, as a part of 咎 (85 of its 90 texts). The text uses 無 and 厲, never 无 or 厉.
  • Yang and yin. A line is solid (yang, a 9 when it moves) or broken (yin, a 6) in the hexagram as it stands, from scripts/hexagram-binary.json.
  • The colour. In the default view a cell is shaded by its share of 吉 minus its share of 凶, fully at 50 points apart. Choose a single word to shade by its own share instead. The pale cells are the near-even ones.
  • The p-values. Exact two-sided Fisher tests on the pairs named above, as a guide to how much a gap could be chance. They are not corrected for the many cuts here and the texts are not independent, so none of them settles anything.

What this does and does not show

It shows where the words fall in the book as written. It does not say what a line means, how a reader should weigh a verdict word against the rest of its text, or what a cast will bring. The same counts for the fifth place are the table in The Changing-Lines Lens.
